The full picture

Abstract

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods are critical for adapting large models under resource-constrained settings. However, as mixture-of-experts (MoE) architectures, which offer efficient inference at massive parameter scales, gain increasing prevalence in the community, a critical gap arises from the lack of systematic recipes for applying PEFT to MoE models.

Read the full abstract +

To narrow this gap, we conduct a large-scale systematic study that investigates a set of essential design recipes, including which modules should be adapted, which granularity should be used to adapt MoE modules, which PEFT method to use, and to what extent the load-balancing objective should be used, across three MoE backbones on downstream tasks and general knowledge tasks.

Our experiments yield several key insights. First, adapting MoE experts alone achieves the highest downstream performance with competitive general capability retention under a comparable parameter budget. Second, native expert-weight updates offer a better downstream–retention trade-off than adapters that bypass experts or entire MoE modules. Third, LoRA and DoRA together define the strongest operating region for high downstream accuracy among the evaluated PEFT methods. Fourth, no nonzero load-balancing coefficient Pareto-dominates the zero baseline.

We finally evaluate a weight-level redesign of MoE²-LoRA on Qwen3. Moving its module-level bypass pool into native expert weights improves all four upstream endpoints by 2.4 to 17.9 points with 17% fewer trainable parameters and a 3.2-point average downstream cost.

The central question

Adapt more.
Forget less.

How should we fine-tune a sparsely activated model without losing the capabilities it already has?

We examine four design choices, evaluating downstream performance and two upstream tasks: knowledge reasoning (MMLU-Pro) and instruction following (IFEval).

WHERE TO ADAPT

Target the experts.

Expert-only adaptation reaches 68.6% mean downstream accuracy, versus 61.7% for attention-only at a comparable budget.

The trade-off: attention + experts retains more general capability in this comparison.
HOW FINELY TO ADAPT

Go inside the weights.

Weight-level rank one reaches 68.5% mean downstream accuracy, with MMLU-Pro at +0.8 pp and IFEval at −1.4 pp relative to the base.

Rank two gains 0.6 downstream points, at roughly twice the trainable parameters and lower retention.
WHICH UPDATE FORM

Start with LoRA or DoRA.

Both reach 68.5% mean downstream accuracy and improve MMLU-Pro by 0.8 pp. DoRA has a slightly smaller IFEval decline.

Retention alone is insufficient: some low-drift methods fail to learn the target task well.
HOW MUCH BALANCING

Keep balancing light.

No nonzero coefficient Pareto-dominates zero. Mean downstream accuracy falls from 68.5% at λ = 0 to 58.6% at λ = 10−1.

A moderate coefficient can improve one retention benchmark while sacrificing downstream accuracy or another capability.

04 / Looking inside

Understanding
weight-level adaptation.

Why updating native weights can do more
than their parameter rank suggests.

Weight-level updates reuse the frozen expert’s nonlinear features. On Qwen3, this produces broad functional responses while preserving alignment with the pretrained expert’s spectral structure.
